# Law of Contract I — DME Semester 1 (2026), Set A

**Source:** Student-supplied photo of the printed internal paper; transcribed for educational analysis without enrolment or identifying marks.

## Section A — short notes
Instruction: Answer **any 3 of 4**, approximately **100 words each**; **3 marks** each (9 total).

- **A.** Explain when the consideration of an agreement becomes unlawful.
- **B.** Discuss the legal position of a person of unsound mind under the Indian Contract Act, 1872.
- **C.** Distinguish between fraud and misrepresentation.
- **D.** Explain the meaning and purpose of quasi-contracts with an example.

## Section B — long answers
Instruction: Answer **any 3 of 4**, **not more than 200 words each**; **7 marks** each (21 total).

- **Q1.** Explain the doctrine of privity of contract. Can a person who is not a party to a contract enforce its terms? Discuss with examples and relevant case law.
- **Q2.** A person aged 17 borrows ₹50,000 while falsely claiming to be 19, spends the money on personal expenses, and refuses to repay. The lender sues. Decide the case using the legal position of minors and relevant case law.
- **Q3.** Define consideration under the Indian Contract Act, 1872. Explain its essential elements and the circumstances where an agreement without consideration is valid.
- **Q4.** A hall rented for a concert is destroyed by accidental fire before the event. The renter demands a replacement hall and damages. Apply the doctrine of frustration under Section 56 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872; explain when supervening impossibility makes a contract void, with examples and relevant authority.

## Pre-exam skills and signals
- **Recall:** unlawful consideration, contractual capacity, fraud versus misrepresentation, quasi-contract.
- **Analysis:** privity, exceptions to consideration, minor's contracts and restitution, frustration versus ordinary commercial difficulty.
- **Answer form:** Issue → Rule → Application → Conclusion for facts; authority or statute only when supported by a reliable source.
- **Original mock design:** use different hypothetical parties, facts and values; test the same legal distinctions without reproducing the exact loan or fire scenarios.
